02 / Tenant isolation

Single-tenant and multitenant selection criteria

Prefer one Microsoft Entra tenant when it meets the mandatory requirements. Document the requirement for each additional tenant or independently managed identity domain.

01

Single-tenant administration

Use shared collaboration and delegated administration where they meet the requirements.

Regulatory labels, local keys, acquisition frequency or an organization-chart difference alone do not establish an unmet isolation requirement.

Separate administration requirements

  • Independent identity or recovery authority
  • Sovereign, legal or regulatory constraints
  • Safety or operation without upstream services
  • Scoped acquisition and divestiture access

Shared identities and cross-tenant administration can retain common authentication or privilege dependencies even when resources are in separate tenants.

Additional tenants need an unmet isolation requirement and an operating model. Sources: MS-02, MS-03, MS-21. Source catalog

04 / Evaluation sequence

Check mandatory constraints before comparing alternatives.

Candidate is a review option, not approval. Unknown mandatory facts mean insufficient evidence; known mandatory conflicts exclude the assessed configuration.

  1. 01

    Mandatory constraints

    Record the required legal, safety, administrative and continuity conditions.

  2. 02

    Authority and dependency analysis

    Trace principals, permissions, integrations and the systems they can affect.

  3. 03

    Failure-scenario testing

    Test compromise, outages, isolation, recovery and organizational change.

  4. 04

    Risk, cost and complexity comparison

    Compare feasible options, including the effort required to change or separate them.

  5. 05

    Operating-capability assessment

    Confirm the staffing, skills, monitoring and recovery procedures required.

Reject infeasible designs before comparing operational tradeoffs. Sources: STD-03, STD-05. Source catalog

05 / Topology patterns

Topology patterns and selection criteria

Decide tenant topology, identity, sovereignty, connectivity and recovery separately. S3 does not imply T3; T4 is a disconnected operating overlay.

T1

One global tenant with delegated boundaries

One tenant meets the requirements, and shared administration is appropriate.

T2

Federated multi-tenant enterprise

Separate control planes with governed cross-boundary collaboration.

T3

Sovereign or independently recoverable tenant

Independent identity authority is required; shared privilege paths must be removed.

T4

Disconnected enclave / cyber-physical cell

S4 local operating pattern; select identity and tenant topology separately.

T5

Acquisition transition zone

Restricted access during assessment, followed by reviewed access expansion and tested separation.

Proposed topology patterns. Sources: MS-02, MS-03, MS-11, MS-18, MS-19, MS-20, STD-02. Read use and rejection criteria

06 / Operation during outages

Functions required during upstream outages

Assign an S0-S4 tier, identify the required local functions and specify the outage duration to test.

S0

Cloud-dependent

Online SaaS and cloud operations.

Central identity, policy and SOC.

S1

Transient outage

Bounded, explicitly tested functions only.

Existing sessions may continue; emergency accounts still depend on Entra.

S2

Extended degraded

Days of reduced function.

Selected local authorization, collection and restore capability.

S3

Independent operations

Required functions without upstream control planes.

Local administration, detection, keys and recovery.

S4

Disconnected / air-gapped

Intentional isolation.

Local identity/PKI, signed updates and offline recovery credentials.

No tier proves availability. Sessions or emergency accounts do not bypass a full Entra outage; PHS backup is not automatic authentication switchover.

Proposed operating tiers. Sources: MS-18, MS-19, MS-20, STD-02. Read tier requirements

08 / Identity and authority

Identity, delegated permissions and audit records

Record the acting identity, delegated permissions and action outcome. Select authentication methods from the privilege, data-protection and physical-safety requirements.

H

Humans

  • Phishing-resistant authentication for privileged access
  • Least privilege and just-in-time elevation
  • Emergency access monitored separately
W

Workloads

  • Managed identities or workload federation
  • Bounded permissions and explicit trust relationships
  • Replace long-lived stored secrets where supported
A

Agents

  • Distinct logical-agent identity, sponsor and technical owner
  • Separate OBO, app-only and agent-user policy targets
  • Least privilege and revocation; API keys bypass Entra CA

Replicas and sessions do not automatically require distinct agent identities. Revalidate licensing and cloud support. Sources: MS-05, MS-09, MS-10, MS-22, STD-04. All 26 controls

09 / Cyber recovery

Recovery credentials and service dependencies

Plan restoration after compromise separately from restoration after a service outage. Identify the credentials and services required by each recovery step.

Circular recovery dependency

Compromised identity↓ Vault and recovery tools↓ Requires that same identity

Independent recovery access

  1. Independent credentials, keys and configuration baselines
  2. Recovery identities, devices and communications isolated from the suspected compromise
  3. Security operations and critical applications
  4. Audit records, acceptance checks and controlled restoration of access

Test the sequence against documented recovery-time and data-loss objectives.

Sources: MS-07, MS-08, MS-26. Bounded recovery runbooks are local review drafts; live lab exercises were not performed and remain blocked.

10 / Assumptions to test

Boundaries do not prove independence

Test what a boundary actually protects and which shared dependencies remain. A tenant, key or network label does not establish the required operating outcome.

Separate tenant ≠ independent identity

Trace shared authentication, cross-tenant administration and indirect privilege paths.

Key control ≠ sovereignty

Assess processing location, operator access, legal jurisdiction and recovery authority separately.

Air gap ≠ secure operation

Provide local detection, controlled updates, audit records and tested recovery procedures.

Integration ≠ enforcement parity

Check cloud-specific coverage, regional limits and response latency; retain native controls where required.

Approve the boundary only when authority, outage behavior and recovery tests demonstrate the required separation.

Sources: MS-02, MS-14, MS-16, MS-17, MS-18. Review design assumptions

11 / Implementation sequence

Check prerequisites before adding control planes.

Protect privileged access and prove initial recovery before expanding scope. Later phases broaden coverage; they do not postpone the first recovery test.

010-90 days

Protect and prove the baseline

  • Phishing-resistant privileged access
  • Test emergency credentials
  • Initial recovery proof and RTO/RPO
023-9 months

Expand only after the gate

  • Owner and baseline proof accepted
  • Policy as code and data controls
  • Review new privilege paths
039-18 months

Broaden local coverage

  • Prove required S2-S4 functions
  • Local SOC and safety authority
  • Acquisition separation tests
0418-36 months

Exercise combined failures

  • Broader isolated recovery
  • Control-plane plus communications loss
  • Drift and complexity reduction

Planning ranges depend on staffing, system criticality and readiness; they are not delivery estimates. Source: STD-05. Read the implementation phases
