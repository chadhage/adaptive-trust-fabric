01 / Position

Selection criteria

Select tenant topology from isolation, sovereignty, continuity, recovery and operating requirements.

Prefer a single Microsoft Entra tenant when it meets the applicable requirements. Document the isolation requirement, dependencies, administration, recovery and separation procedures for each additional tenant or identity domain.

Selection rule

Reject designs that violate a mandatory requirement. Compare risk, cost and operating complexity only among feasible alternatives.

02 / Reference architecture

Policy, identity, enforcement and recovery responsibilities

The design separates policy ownership, identity administration, enforcement, telemetry and recovery. Microsoft cloud services provide supported functions; cloud-provider and local controls implement requirements outside that coverage.

    03 / Interactive assessment

    Match topology to operating requirements

    Start with mandatory identity, sovereignty, continuity and recovery constraints. Treat a topology as a design candidate, not deployment approval: verify authority, dependencies, staffing, legal and safety requirements, and failure-scenario results before implementation.

    Design decision

    Match the boundary to the requirement.

    A viable topology must meet mandatory requirements and sustain the selected outage duration with sufficient operating and recovery capability.

    04 / Operation during outages

    S0-S4 operating requirements

    The tiers specify which identity, authorization, detection, update, cryptographic and recovery functions must remain available when upstream services are unavailable. Test the required functions and outage duration in the target environment.

    05 / Topology patterns

    T1-T5 topology criteria

    06 / Control requirements

    MUST / SHOULD / MAY architecture controls

    Translate isolation, access, safety and recovery obligations into owned, testable controls. MUST denotes a required control in the proposed architecture; SHOULD recommends and MAY permits an option. Assess applicable vendor and legal requirements separately.

    07 / Assumption review

    Documented limitations and their design implications

    Challenge assumptions about identity isolation, key control, monitoring and disconnected operation. Verify actual authority paths and failure behavior rather than relying on tenant, product or network labels.

    08 / Transition

    Proposed implementation sequence

    Use the phases to identify prerequisites and assign work. Review staffing, monitoring, incident response and recovery capability before adding control planes. The durations are planning ranges, not delivery estimates.

    09 / Supporting guidance

    Standards and product guidance

    Validate isolation, authentication, safety and recovery requirements against applicable standards and current product capabilities. Confirm local legal obligations, service coverage and operating constraints before deployment.

    Deployment review

    Document requirements and test operating procedures.

    Review isolation, administrative dependencies, outage behavior and recovery procedures for the target environment. Re-evaluate the topology when requirements, dependencies or operating capabilities change.
